CHATin — AI study coach for the ENEM exam

Partner company
GSW
My role
Product Owner
Period
Sep – Nov 2026

Problem

Students preparing for the ENEM (Brazil's national university entrance exam) struggle to know their real level in each subject, to decide what to study next given the huge syllabus, and to notice their own progress. Personal tutoring is expensive, feedback on mistakes usually comes late, and the lack of visible progress leads students to abandon their study plans. On the partner's side there is also a cost constraint: AI usage has to be measured and capped, and the system must stay useful even when the AI is unavailable.

Solution

A web application where students chat freely with an AI coach. The system automatically recognizes the ENEM subject and topic being discussed, generates summaries saved to a private library, creates quizzes with answer keys and automatic grading, tracks progress per topic and adjusts the difficulty of upcoming quizzes. It includes a study-material area (a guided handbook), student-built study tracks, web source search with citations, and gamification (XP, streaks and a nickname-based ranking).

  • Tailwind CSS

  • Built the Product Backlog with 20 user stories, with priority, estimates and acceptance criteria, split into 3 sprints.
  • Defined the project's DoR and DoD and kept the README and sprint reports up to date for partner reviews.

Themis — AI-powered legal precedent analysis

Partner company
Xertica
My role
Dev Team · Mobile app
Period
Mar – May 2026

Problem

Lawyers and judges must ground petitions and rulings on precedents, but court decisions are spread across many courts and come in huge volumes. Finding the precedents that truly apply means reading entire decisions with no objective measure of how similar they are to the case, which takes a lot of time and increases the risk of citing an inappropriate or outdated precedent.

Solution

A mobile app connected to a Python API that analyzes the petition (PDF or case description) with natural language processing, queries an indexed precedent database and returns related decisions with a similarity percentage, an applicability rating (applicable / possibly applicable / not applicable), a case summary and an explanation of how each precedent relates to the case. In lawyer mode, the AI also drafts the initial petition from the case description.

Technologies

  • Flutter
  • Dart
  • flutter_hooks
  • Python
  • FastAPI
  • MongoDB
  • LLM / NLP
  • JWT
  • Docker
  • Figma

Personal contributions

I was the top committer in the app repository.

  • Login and sign-up screens, app routing and integration of the petition upload screen.
  • Track 1 (lawyer mode): petition generation screen with a court filter (STF, STJ, TST, TRF, TRT…) and asynchronous polling integration with the API.
  • Result screen with the petition rendered in Markdown and a precedent list with relevance badges and similarity %; a bottom sheet to edit and regenerate the petition.
  • Petition summaries, petition history, draft rulings and PDF export.
  • JWT authentication with the FastAPI back-end and a final QA/bug-fix round before delivery.

Hard skills developed

  • Mobile development with Flutter/Dart and reactive state with flutter_hooks.
  • Consuming asynchronous APIs (polling) and handling loading and error states.
  • Markdown rendering and PDF generation inside the app.

Soft skills practiced

Adaptability
I learned Flutter during the project — new to me and to most of the team — and still delivered the Track 1 screens within the sprints.
Autonomy
I made UX and screen-structure decisions without needing constant sign-off, which reduced rework.
Teamwork
I aligned API contracts, the polling flow and error messages with the teammates building the Python back-end.

SkyTrack — Weather monitoring and risk alerts

Partner company
Tecsus
My role
Dev Team · Front-end
Period
Sep – Nov 2025

Problem

The client had no real-time access to rainfall, wind, humidity, temperature and pressure data, which made it hard to anticipate risks such as floods and landslides. In addition, stations and sensors from different manufacturers use different formats, preventing the data from being gathered in one place, and the existing information was not understandable to a lay audience (students and residents).

Solution

A platform that receives data from low-cost weather stations (ESP32 microcontroller with sensors), with a dynamic data model that accepts different station and parameter types. Data is shown on a dashboard with history and reports; alert rules trigger automatic risk notifications; and an educational area explains, in plain language, how measurements and estimates are calculated.

  • Replaced mock data with the real back-end integration.
  • Alert notifications for public users and the Education page.
  • Dark mode, visual standardization and folder-structure refactoring.
  • CI pipeline on GitHub Actions (front-end build) and integration tests with Jest.

Hard skills developed

  • Time-series visualization and data export.
  • Continuous integration (GitHub Actions) and automated testing with Jest.

NectoPoint — Employee time tracking

Partner company
Necto Systems
My role
Dev Team · Front-end
Period
Mar – May 2025

Problem

Manual time recording caused errors and inconsistencies in hours worked, and there was no single place to handle overtime, vacations, days off and absence justifications. As a result, employee requests and manager approvals were scattered, and decisions about hour banks and schedules were made without reliable data.

Solution

A web time-tracking system: employees clock in, clock out and log breaks, check their history and open requests (vacation, day off, overtime, excused absence with an attached medical certificate, time adjustment). Managers approve or reject requests, adjust records and follow overtime and absence reports, with automatic calculations compliant with Brazilian labor law (CLT).
